Primary request: Generate a NEW complete illustration from scratch, using Image 1 solely as a visual style and framing reference. Build new shapes and clean new surfaces; do not retouch, trace or reuse the reference's textures.

Image 1 role: STYLE AND COMPOSITION reference only. Use a similar left-facing sagittal cutaway of the mouth and neck with a single round lower-left magnified inset. Its highlighted glottis and closed vocal-fold inset belong to a different letter and must not be carried over.
Anatomical basis: the Quranpedia throat diagram distinguishes the middle throat around the epiglottis from the deeper true vocal-fold area. This image illustrates only the middle throat/epiglottic region used for ح.

Main view: Left-facing sagittal cutaway showing the mouth, relaxed tongue, pharynx, upright epiglottis, larynx and enough upper neck to orient the learner. Lips and teeth gently apart, tongue contained in the mouth, tip relaxed behind the lower front teeth with no dental or palate contact. Show a modest narrowing of the pharyngeal airway at the level of the epiglottis, below the tongue root and ABOVE the true vocal folds. The airway remains visibly open through this narrowing, representing continued air flow during ح. The epiglottis is upright, not folded across the airway. A small restrained amber highlight identifies that middle-throat narrowing beside the upper epiglottis and opposing posterior pharyngeal wall. The lower glottis remains unhighlighted and open.

Use a thin amber circle and one fine leader joining it to the correct middle-throat highlight. No top-down closed vocal folds, no central sealed seam. Anatomically consistent with the main view.
Finish: clean sharp contours, coherent soft shading, smooth soft-tissue surfaces with very few fine anatomical curves. Simple enough to read on a phone. Portrait near-square composition. Small warm amber highlights only; no excessive bloom.
No text, letters, numbers, labels, legends, arrows, watermarks, extra panels, closed throat, tongue protrusion, fuzzy patches or copied source-image defects.
